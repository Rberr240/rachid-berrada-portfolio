# Discovery Call — Script & Checklist

Internal reference. Used at the MEETING stage of [SALES_PLAYBOOK.md](../SALES_PLAYBOOK.md). Goal: understand the business and the real problem, confirm fit, and gather enough information to write an accurate proposal — not to pitch or price anything on the call itself.

Target length: 20–30 minutes. Keep the tone consistent with [BRAND_SYSTEM.md](../BRAND_SYSTEM.md): direct, technical, confident, genuinely curious about the business — never scripted-sounding or pushy.

## 1. Opening (2–3 minutes)

Goals: set a relaxed tone, confirm time available, frame the call as a two-way fit check.

Suggested opening:

> "Thanks for making the time. Before anything else — how much time do you have today? I want to make sure we cover what matters most to you."
>
> "My goal for this call is simple: understand what's happening in your business right now, where things are getting in the way, and whether I can genuinely help. If it turns out I'm not the right fit, I'll tell you that directly."
>
> "Would it be alright if I ask a few questions first to understand the situation, and then we can talk about what a solution might look like?"

## 2. Qualification questions

Establish the business context and confirm this is a real opportunity before going deep on scope.

- "Can you tell me a bit about the business — what you do, and who your customers are?"
- "What made you reach out / take this call now? Was there a specific moment or event that triggered this?"
- "How are things handled today — [booking / leads / customer contact / the specific process in question]?"
- "Who else, if anyone, is involved in deciding to move forward with something like this?"
- "Have you worked with a developer or agency before? How did that go?"
- "Roughly, what would success look like for you six months from now?"

## 3. Scope-probing questions

Dig into the specific problem and what a solution needs to include.

- "Walk me through what happens today, step by step, when a [customer/client/patient] wants to [book / buy / contact you]."
- "Where in that process do things break down, slow down, or get missed?"
- "What tools are you using today — website, booking system, CRM, spreadsheets, WhatsApp, anything else?"
- "Is there anything that absolutely has to stay the way it is — a tool you're attached to, a process your team already knows?"
- "If I built exactly what you're picturing, what would it need to do on day one?"
- "Is there anything you've seen elsewhere — another business, a competitor, an app — that's close to what you have in mind?"
- "Who will actually be using this day to day, once it's live?"

## 4. Budget and timeline questions

Ask directly but comfortably — the goal is a workable range, not an exact number.

- "Have you thought about what kind of budget range makes sense for this? Even a rough range helps me propose something realistic rather than guessing."
- "Is there a specific date or event this needs to be ready for, or is the timeline flexible?"
- "If I come back with a proposal, what would your process be for reviewing and approving it?"
- "Is this something you're planning to fund yourself, or does it need approval from someone else first?"

If the client is uncomfortable giving a number: "That's fine — even telling me if we're talking about hundreds, low thousands, or more helps me scope this sensibly for you."

## 5. Closing / next-steps script

Goals: confirm what was heard, set expectations for the proposal, and end with a clear, specific next step.

> "This is really helpful, thank you. Just to make sure I've got it right — [summarize the problem and objective in one or two sentences back to the client]. Does that sound right?"
>
> "Here's what I'll do next: I'll put together a written proposal covering the approach, what's included, the timeline, and the investment. You should have that by [specific day, e.g. 'Thursday' or 'within 3 business days']."
>
> "Once you've had a look, we can jump on a quick call or go back and forth by message — whatever's easier for you — to talk through any questions."
>
> "Does that work for you? And is [email/WhatsApp] the best way to send it over?"

Confirm before ending the call:
- [ ] Best contact channel and address/number confirmed for sending the proposal.
- [ ] A specific day (not "soon") given for when the proposal will arrive.
- [ ] Client knows what to expect in the proposal (approach, scope, timeline, investment).
- [ ] Any immediate red flags (Section 4 of [SALES_PLAYBOOK.md](../SALES_PLAYBOOK.md)) noted for internal review before drafting the proposal.

## 6. Post-call checklist (internal, not read aloud)

Immediately after the call, before moving to PROPOSAL stage:

- [ ] Problem statement written down in the client's own words.
- [ ] Objectives captured (what changes for the business if this succeeds).
- [ ] Scope notes captured against the [Scope checklist in SALES_PLAYBOOK.md](../SALES_PLAYBOOK.md#5-scope-checklist).
- [ ] Budget range and timeline expectation logged, even if approximate.
- [ ] Decision-maker(s) confirmed.
- [ ] Qualification checklist re-checked — proceed to proposal, ask a clarifying follow-up message, or mark LOST with a reason.
